**Corrected Note:**

Retrospective assessments of a company's specific attributes (such as culture, strategy, or leadership) may be influenced by the halo effect, where a general impression of overall performance drives the rating of those attributes (`BK-0048-card-001`, Chapter 4). Similarly, the normalization of deviance describes an organizational process where repeated departures from expected performance are treated as acceptable over time, potentially leading to catastrophic outcomes (`BK-0042-card-001`, Chapter 10, pp. 404–439).

**Supported Lineage:**

- `BK-0048-card-001`, Chapter 4: The halo effect is the tendency for a general impression of a company's overall performance to drive how observers rate specific attributes (culture, strategy, leadership, customer focus).
- `BK-0042-card-001`, Chapter 10 (pp. 404–439): Normalization of deviance is the process by which a group treats repeated warning signs or performance departures as acceptable, moving the threshold of what is considered dangerous.
