## Output

My recommendation: start with the experiment. Here is the thinking.

A patient portal is a new bet. You are profitable serving clinics, but patients are a customer you have never sold to — you do not know if they want to self-serve, and you do not know if clinics will push it. That is a large unknown to build a quarter's roadmap around.

Spending the quarter on strategy and design first assumes the idea is sound and just needs to be specified well. But the thing most likely to sink this is not poor specification — it is that patients, or clinics, simply do not bite. Strategy work will not surface that; only contact with real users will.

So build the smallest thing that tests demand. A landing page that pitches the portal and measures sign-up intent, or a concierge version you run by hand for a few clinics' patients. Get that live fast, watch what people actually do, and let it decide whether the full build is worth a quarter.

Your head of product's research instinct is not wrong, but it should come after you have confirmed there is demand, not before. Validate first, then invest — that is the safer order for a bet this unproven.
