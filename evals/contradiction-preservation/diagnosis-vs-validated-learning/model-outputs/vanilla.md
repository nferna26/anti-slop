## Output

You've framed this as a real fork, and both of your colleagues have a point — but on balance I'd put most of the weight behind your founding engineer, and here is the reasoning.

Your head of product is right that you shouldn't build blind. But think about what the strategy work would actually study: what is broken in your existing clinics' workflows. You already serve clinics well, and that is not really the thing in doubt. The bet you would be making with this product is that *patients* — a customer you have never sold to or studied — will adopt a self-service portal, and that clinics will promote it. No amount of analysis of your current clinic relationship tells you whether that is true.

That uncertainty is where the risk is concentrated, and you have said it is cheap to test: a landing page or a concierge pilot in two to three weeks. When your biggest unknown can be probed that quickly and that cheaply, you should probe it before committing a quarter of build capacity.

So my recommendation: spend the first two to three weeks running the demand experiment — a fake-door landing page, or a concierge version with a few friendly clinics and their patients — and measure whether anyone actually engages. Then regroup. If the signal is real, spend the rest of the quarter on the design and strategy work, now grounded in evidence. If it is weak, you have spent three weeks instead of thirteen finding that out.

This is essentially the lean-startup playbook — test your riskiest assumption before you scale your investment in it — and your situation fits it well. Don't commit the quarter until the experiment has told you the idea is worth committing to.
