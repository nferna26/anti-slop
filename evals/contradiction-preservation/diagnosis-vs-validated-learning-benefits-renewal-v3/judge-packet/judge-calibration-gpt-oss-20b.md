## Method

Per `case.md` → `## Judge protocol`, calibration runs **before** any `OUT-NN`
scoring. The judge scored each of the three Surface-1 calibration anchors
against `rubric.md` (criteria C1–C6 plus the criterion dependency rule), in its
own independent call, with no reference verdicts supplied. Only after the
judge's verdicts were recorded were they compared against the Surface 2
reference verdicts in `judge-packet/calibration-anchors.md`.

## Eligibility result

The pre-registered calibration gate (`calibration-anchors.md` →
`## Judge eligibility rule`): a judge is eligible only if its anchor verdicts
differ from the reference by **no more than two criteria total** across all
three anchors **and** it does **not** disagree with the reference on **C5 or
C6** for any anchor.

- Total criteria differences across the three anchors: **2** (within the
  ≤2 count).
- C5/C6 disagreements: **2 — Anchor C C5 and Anchor C C6** (the gate allows
  none).

**`gpt-oss:20b` is NOT eligible.** It fails the C5/C6 clause of the calibration
gate. Per the `## Judge protocol`, a miscalibrated judge does not score the real
outputs and does not count toward the two-judge minimum for any `## Result` lift
above `partial` unless the operator explicitly accepts it with a recorded
limitation. Accordingly, **this judge scored none of the forty `OUT-NN`
outputs**; scoring was stopped at the calibration gate.

## Interpretation

This is the v3 calibration gate working as designed. The gate exists to screen
out judges miscalibrated on the C5/C6 boundary — the exact axis on which the
two v2 judges diverged (v2 judges disagreed on C5 for 20/40 and C6 for 22/40
outputs). `gpt-oss:20b` reads a hybrid answer that does not divide the tradeoff
as still clearing C5/C6; the v3 reference does not. Catching that **before**
scoring is the point of the gate. A future v3 judge pass needs either a judge
that clears the calibration gate, or an explicit operator decision to accept a
miscalibrated judge with a recorded limitation.

## Limitation

This calibration was a **local-model judge run orchestrated by the eval agent** —
not a fully independent third-party or human evaluation. The case's
`## Judge protocol` requires at least two eligible blind judges from different
model families, at least one independent of the orchestrating agent, before any
`## Result` lift above `partial`. This attempt produced **zero** eligible
judges.
