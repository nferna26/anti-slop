# Output OUT-04

**Recommendation:** Recommend the deputy director’s approach: decline the appropriation and run the three four-week, reversible tests (document reminders, callback scheduler, and a two-person navigator prototype) using ordinary operating funds.

**Strongest reason against it:** The opportunity cost of the funding. By declining the appropriation, you lose a significant, one-time injection of capital that cannot be recovered for a year. If the "handoff confusion" hypothesis is correct and the scale of the problem requires a massive resource surge to fix, you have traded a definitive solution for a series of small experiments, potentially leaving residents in a state of failure for another twelve months.

**Observable evidence that would change my mind:** Data showing that the drop-off occurs specifically and predominantly at the handoff points, rather than at the new login step or during the document checklist phase. If a "leakage report" showed that residents are completing the login and the checklist but vanishing specifically during the routing process, the risk of the full navigator program would be justified by the certainty of the diagnosis.
