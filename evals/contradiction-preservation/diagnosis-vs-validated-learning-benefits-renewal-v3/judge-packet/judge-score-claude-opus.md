## Read this first — independence and calibration limitations

This pass carries three limitations, and together they mean it **cannot by
itself support any `## Result` lift** and does **not** count toward the case's
independent-judge requirement.

1. **Not independent of the orchestrator.** Claude Opus 4.7 is the same agent
   that designed the case, wrote the rubric, froze the packets, ran the
   `gemma4:31b` generator pass, and built this judge packet. The case's
   `## Judge protocol` requires an eligible judge that is *not* the orchestrating
   agent before any Result lift; this judge does not meet that bar.
2. **The calibration is circular.** The calibration gate is meant to verify that
   a judge reads C5/C6 the way the reference does. Claude Opus 4.7 **authored the
   three calibration anchors, the Surface 2 reference verdicts, and the rubric
   itself**. Its calibration verdicts match the reference exactly — but that
   match demonstrates self-consistency, not calibration. The calibration gate
   provides **no independent assurance** for this judge.
3. **Partial exposure during orchestration.** While orchestrating the run, the
   judge saw a small number of condition-labelled raw model outputs. For any
   `OUT-NN` whose text the judge recognises, the condition-blind property is not
   guaranteed. The judge did **not** read the `OUT-NN` → condition answer key or
   the `model-outputs/` receipts, and scored every output against the rubric
   regardless of any condition it could infer.

Because of (1)-(3), this receipt is recorded as a **judge-variance data point**
only. A genuine v3 judge pass still needs an eligible judge that is independent
of the orchestrator and that did not author the rubric or the calibration
reference.

C1 (identifies the tension) and C2 (anchors in facts) pass on nearly every output — the Advisor prompt's request for a recommendation, a reason against, and disconfirming evidence pulls every answer to at least gesture at both. The discrimination is carried by C3 (preserve the diagnosis-first objection) and C4 (detect and connect the under-evidence problem): answers with full reasoning that hold the tension clear C3-C6 and score 6/6; terse template answers fail C3, and either connect the under-evidence gap to the commitment (C4 pass → 3/6) or do not (C4 fail → 2/6). One answer was too terse to surface the diagnose-and-act reading at all (1/6).

## What this receipt is not

This is one judge's blind scoring of the forty answers, by a judge that is the orchestrating agent, authored the rubric and the calibration anchors/reference, and is therefore neither independent nor independently calibrated. It is not an eval `## Result`, and it does not move the case toward `benchmark_supported`. It does **not** reconcile `OUT-NN` to conditions, compare conditions, or draw any conclusion about the eval's hypothesis. The case `## Result` stays `partial` and `scoring_status` stays `unscored`. A model output, and a judge's score of one, is a test artifact — never an authority.
