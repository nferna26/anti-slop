# Output OUT-22

**Recommendation**
Recommend that the CEO commit to the **targeted evidence pass**. This involves pausing new discounts, instrumenting reviewer-level behavior by segment, testing auditor-explainability in the insurance queue, and running a control group against the strategic-feature pitch.

**Reasoning**
The current "upbeat story" is a textbook instance of the **halo effect** (BK-0048-card-001). The sudden shift in how leadership describes the team—from "bespoke and reactive" to "customer-obsessed and disciplined"—is not based on independent evidence of improved managerial traits, but is a reflection of a temporary revenue bump. This "performance signal" has tinted the perception of the product's success, creating a dangerous illusion of product-market fit.

From a structural standpoint, the proposal to "scale broadly" (Option 1) is not a strategy; it is a list of initiatives lacking a valid **diagnosis** (BK-0001-card-001). The current diagnosis—that Review Assist is a "market wedge"—is based on vanity metrics. The revenue increase is largely artificial (72% driven by discounts), and usage is skewed toward "champions" rather than the front-line reviewers who actually derive value from the tool.

To avoid committing the company to a flawed path, HelioLedger must shift its measure of progress from execution targets (revenue and shipment) to **validated learning** (BK-0007-card-001). Real progress is not the 18% revenue increase, but empirical evidence of how actual customers behave. Currently, the evidence suggests the product is failing its primary users (31% return rate) and is unusable in regulated segments due to a lack of explainability. A targeted evidence pass is the only way to separate the "halo" of the revenue bump from the reality of the product's utility.

**The Strongest Reason Against**
The primary risk is **political and temporal**. The board locks the budget in twelve days and is currently primed for an "upbeat story." Recommending a pause on discounts and a shift toward a rigorous evidence pass may be perceived by the CEO and the board as a lack of confidence or a "loss of momentum" at a critical funding juncture. By rejecting the "revenue-led annual plan," you are sacrificing the short-term optics of aggressive growth for long-term structural stability.

**Observable Evidence That Would Change My Mind**
I would shift my recommendation to "scale broadly" only if the **front-line reviewer retention rate** (currently 31%) showed a statistically significant increase in accounts where the "strategic-feature pitch" was *not* delivered and discounts were *not* applied. If the product's value is strong enough to drive adoption and retention independently of the sales narrative and financial incentives, the "market wedge" diagnosis would be validated, and the halo effect would be replaced by independent evidence of product-market fit.
