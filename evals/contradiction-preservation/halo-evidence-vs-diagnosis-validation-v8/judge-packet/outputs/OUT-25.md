# Output OUT-25

**Recommendation**
Recommend that the CEO commit to the **targeted evidence pass**.

**Reasoning**
The current "upbeat story" presented to the board is a textbook example of the **halo effect** (BK-0048-card-001). The sudden rise in net revenue retention and the shift in leadership’s perception of the team—from "bespoke and reactive" to "disciplined and clear"—is not based on independent evidence of improved strategy or culture. Instead, it is a reflection of a positive performance signal (the revenue bump). This signal is itself artificial, as 72% of the expansion revenue is driven by short-term discounts rather than organic value creation.

Furthermore, the company is currently confusing execution targets (NRR, renewals) with **validated learning** (BK-0007-card-001). While the board sees "progress," the empirical evidence from customer behavior suggests the opposite: front-line reviewer retention is abysmal (31%), and the product is being abandoned in critical regulated segments due to a lack of explainability.

Scaling Review Assist broadly right now would be a catastrophic error because the company lacks a **strategy kernel** (BK-0001-card-001). There is no honest *diagnosis* of why the product is (or isn't) working; there is only a set of "coherent actions" (quota expansion, hiring) being applied to a hallucinated success. The targeted evidence pass is the only option that treats the current situation as a hypothesis to be tested rather than a victory to be scaled.

**Strongest Reason Against**
The budget locks in twelve days. A targeted evidence pass requires pausing the very discounts and "strategic pitches" that are currently inflating the revenue numbers and pleasing the board. By choosing this path, you are intentionally slowing down the "momentum" and risking the CEO's standing with a board that is currently under the influence of a performance-driven halo.

**Observable Evidence that would change my mind**
I would shift my recommendation to "Scale Broadly" only if the **front-line reviewer retention rate** (currently 31%) showed a significant, organic increase in accounts that *did not* receive the early-renewal discount or the "top priority" pitch. If the users themselves—not the "champions" or the account managers—demonstrate a behavioral shift toward repeat usage without financial or narrative incentives, it would indicate that the product has achieved genuine product-market fit, independent of the halo effect.
