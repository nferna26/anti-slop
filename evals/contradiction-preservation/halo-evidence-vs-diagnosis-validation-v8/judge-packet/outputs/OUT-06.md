# Output OUT-06

**Recommendation**
Recommend that the CEO commit to the **targeted evidence pass**. Specifically, you must pause new discounts, instrument reviewer-level behavior by segment, test auditor-explainability in the insurance queue, and compare adoption against a control group that did not receive the "strategic-feature" pitch.

**Why?**
The current narrative of success is a textbook example of the **Halo Effect (BK-0048-card-001)**. The upbeat board story—claiming the team is now "customer-obsessed" and "disciplined"—is not independent evidence of improved organizational trait or strategy. Instead, these positive labels are being driven by a general impression of success created by the rise in net revenue retention. This is confirmed by the fact that the same behaviors were described as "too bespoke" and "reactive" six months ago; the behaviors haven't changed, only the revenue signal has.

Furthermore, the company is confusing execution metrics with **validated learning (BK-0007-card-001)**. The revenue bump is artificially inflated (72% tied to discounts), and the usage spike is a vanity metric driven by a small group of "champions" rather than the front-line reviewers, of whom only 31% return. To scale Review Assist now would be to build a "revenue-led annual plan" on a foundation of noise.

From a structural perspective, scaling the product without this evidence pass would result in **"Bad Strategy" (BK-0001-card-001)**. A real strategy requires a **kernel** starting with a **diagnosis**. Currently, HelioLedger lacks a valid diagnosis; it has a set of "coherent actions" (quota expansion, hires) but no guiding policy based on a true understanding of why the product is failing in regulated segments or why front-line retention is so low. The evidence pass is the only way to generate a diagnosis that isn't halo-tinted.

**The Strongest Reason Against**
The primary risk is **political and financial friction**. Pausing discounts and delaying a "revenue-led" expansion twelve days before the board locks the budget is a high-friction move. The CEO and Sales leadership may view this as "analysis paralysis" or a failure to capitalize on momentum, potentially risking the short-term revenue targets the board is currently celebrating.

**Observable Evidence That Would Change My Mind**
I would shift my recommendation to "Scale Broadly" if the evidence pass reveals that the 31% front-line retention is a **purely technical enablement issue** (i.e., users who receive a specific 15-minute training module show 80%+ retention) AND if the control group (those who didn't get the "strategic pitch") shows a statistically significant increase in usage and retention compared to the baseline. This would prove that the value proposition is real and the "halo" is actually reflecting a genuine product-market fit rather than a discount-driven mirage.
