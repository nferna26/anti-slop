## Claim

The halo-effect warning in BK-0048 should not be treated as a universal
objection to all positive trait evidence about a successful organisation. The
boundary is whether the observer's trait assessment was formed after, and in
light of, the organisation's known performance. Trait evidence gathered before
the outcome was known, or gathered by raters blind to the outcome signal, is not
halo-contaminated by this mechanism merely because later performance turned out
well. It may still be weak for other reasons, but the BK-0048 halo label needs
the performance-aware trait-recoding path.

## Paraphrase

The Chapter 4 mechanism carded in `BK-0048-card-001` concerns performance-aware
trait perception: once observers know a company's overall result, descriptions
of its culture, leadership, strategy, or customer focus tend to be brought into
line with that result. This boundary card sharpens the negative side of that
claim. The mechanism is not triggered simply because the company later became
successful, or because a later analyst is tempted to tell a success story. The
load-bearing question is when and how the trait evidence was produced. If
ratings were made before the outcome signal was available, or by raters who did
not know which team later performed well, then the evidence is outside the
specific BK-0048 halo mechanism. A recommendation may still ask whether the
measure is valid, reliable, representative, or causally connected to the
outcome; it should not call that evidence halo-tainted without showing that the
performance signal shaped the trait judgment.

## Scope conditions

Applies to trait evidence about organisations where the key question is whether
the evidence is independent of known performance. It excludes cases where the
trait evidence was collected before outcome knowledge, by raters blind to the
outcome, or from artifacts that can be evaluated without revealing which
organisation later succeeded. It does not certify the evidence as causally
strong; it only says the BK-0048 halo mechanism is not the right objection
unless performance-aware recoding is shown.

## Misuse risk

Used to launder favourable trait evidence as valid merely because it was called
"blind" or "pre-outcome" without checking whether raters could infer the
performance signal through other cues. The opposite misuse is also likely:
calling any positive trait evidence about a successful firm "halo" even when the
ratings were genuinely independent of the outcome signal.
