# Claim / Tension Card

## Tension

Two operator-reviewed source cards each locate the source of a unit's results in a different place, and pull against each other on where to look when results disappoint. `BK-0002-card-001` carries Grove's definition: a manager's output is the output of the organisational units the manager supervises and influences — an account that locates a manager's accountable contribution *inside* the firm, in the completed work of the teams the manager runs and shapes. `BK-0023-card-001` carries Porter's mechanism: an industry's long-run profit potential — the ceiling on the returns firms in it can sustain — is set by the combined strength of five structural forces *external* to any one firm. The question this card holds open: when a firm or unit underperforms, or when an advisor is asked why returns are low and what to do, how should the account divide between a manager's accountable output and the industry's structural profit potential — and are these two different levels of analysis, competing explanations of the same shortfall, or sequential diagnostics where one bounds the other?

## Claim A

`BK-0002-card-001` supports the view that the part of a unit's results a manager is accountable for is located internally — in the output of the organisational units the manager runs and influences. On Grove's account, carded from Chapter 3 of *High Output Management*, a manager's output is not the manager's own personal activity but the output of the units the manager supervises, together with the neighbouring units the manager influences without supervising. The locus of accountable managerial contribution is therefore the completed work of those teams: when one asks what a manager has produced, the answer is found in what the supervised and influenced units achieve. This is a definitional claim about what to count as a manager's output; it is internal to the firm and to the manager's operating reach.

## Source cards for Claim A

- `BK-0002-card-001` — a manager's output is the output of the organisational units the manager supervises and influences, not the manager's personal activity (BK-0002, Grove, *High Output Management*, Chapter 3); reviewed source card. Carries the claim that grounds Claim A.

## Claim B

`BK-0023-card-001` supports the view that the ceiling on the returns achievable in an arena is set externally, by the structure of the industry. On Porter's account, carded from Chapter 1 of *Competitive Strategy*, an industry's long-run profit potential is governed by the combined strength of five structural forces — the threat of new entry, the threat of substitutes, the bargaining power of buyers, the bargaining power of suppliers, and rivalry among existing firms. That combined strength fixes how profitable the industry can be over the long run, and industries differ durably in profitability because the strength of their forces differs. This is a claim about the *industry's* profit potential — the arena's structural ceiling — not about any one firm's results, which the source card records depend additionally on the firm's position within the industry.

## Source cards for Claim B

- `BK-0023-card-001` — the combined strength of five structural forces sets an industry's long-run profit potential (BK-0023, Porter, *Competitive Strategy*, Chapter 1); reviewed source card. Carries the claim that grounds Claim B.

## Nature of the tension

This is a level-of-analysis and attribution tension, not a flat contradiction. The two source cards are not mutually exclusive statements about one thing — both can hold at once — but each names a different locus for the source of results and a different unit of analysis. `BK-0002-card-001` is pitched at the manager and the units the manager runs: an account internal to the firm of what a manager's output consists of. `BK-0023-card-001` is pitched at the industry: an account, external to any one firm, of the profit ceiling the arena's structure sets. The two cards also carry different `claim_type`s — `BK-0002-card-001` is `factual` (a definitional reframing of what a manager's output is), `BK-0023-card-001` is `mechanism` (how industry structure produces an industry's profitability) — so the card pairs a definition of accountable managerial output against a mechanism for industry profit potential, and that cross-type pairing is part of why the relationship is unsettled rather than a clean contradiction.

The genuinely open question is what to do with the two when a unit's results disappoint or when advice is framed. Three readings are live: (a) they are *different levels of analysis* — "what is this manager's accountable output" and "what return can this arena structurally sustain" are different questions, and an account that needs both should keep them separate rather than merge them; (b) they are *competing explanations* of the same shortfall — the disappointing result was caused by weak managerial output, or by a structurally unprofitable arena, and the answer changes what should be done; or (c) they are *sequential diagnostics* — one is run first to bound the other, for example a structural pass establishing the band of returns the arena can yield, after which managerial output is assessed against that band. The card does not choose among (a), (b), and (c).

## Deciding conditions

- **Toward foregrounding managerial output and accountability.** The question is whether a particular manager or team is contributing what it should, and comparable units in the same industry — facing roughly the same structure — vary in their results. Where the arena is approximately held constant and the variance is across units, `BK-0002-card-001`'s account of accountable managerial output is the one that discriminates.
- **Toward foregrounding industry structure and profit potential.** The question is about the arena itself — whether a given level of return is achievable here at all, or whether to be in this industry — or industry-wide returns have moved together across firms, which points to a structural force rather than one manager's output. Where the question is the arena rather than a specific manager's contribution, `BK-0023-card-001`'s structural account fits.
- **Toward treating structure as bounding managerial output.** Industry structure sets a ceiling on the returns the arena can sustain; within that ceiling, where a unit lands is the part an account of managerial output speaks to. On this reading a structural pass bounds what even strong managerial output can deliver — an able manager in a structurally poor arena and a weak manager in a structurally rich one are different cases — and the two are run in sequence rather than weighed as rivals.
- **The level test.** When "what return can this arena structurally sustain" and "is this manager's accountable output what it should be" are recognisably two questions, the two cards are answering at different levels, and an advisor that collapses them into one undifferentiated "why are the results bad" is flattening the tension, not resolving it.

## Tension preserved

The tension is preserved as `open`. This card does not decide whether a disappointing result should be attributed to weak managerial output or to a structurally limited arena, and it does not decide whether the two accounts are different levels of analysis, competing explanations, or sequential diagnostics. It records that one reviewed source card locates a manager's accountable output internally — the output of the units the manager supervises and influences — and another locates an industry's long-run profit ceiling externally, in the structure of the arena, and that how to divide an account of results, or frame advice, when both may be load-bearing is unsettled. Flattening this into "both matter" would discard exactly the question the card exists to hold: how the two divide, which to foreground when, and whether one bounds the other.

## Scope conditions

This card's synthesis is bounded by what the two source cards carry. `BK-0002-card-001` is the Chapter 3 definition of a manager's output from *High Output Management* — not performance appraisal, not managerial leverage, and not a claim that the manager determines the result single-handedly. `BK-0023-card-001` is the Chapter 1 five-forces mechanism for *industry-level* long-run profit potential from *Competitive Strategy* — not a single firm's performance, and not the generic strategies or competitor analysis the book treats later; that source card itself records that a firm's own results depend additionally on its position within the industry. The two cards operate at different units of analysis — the manager and the units the manager runs, versus the industry — and that difference is part of the tension, so a reader should not treat them as competing accounts of one variable. The card pairs a definition against a mechanism and reaches no claim beyond those two cards.

## Canon implication

This card could later feed a canon candidate — most naturally a guardrail on how an AI advisory answer should attribute underperformance and frame turnaround advice: not defaulting to a single locus (blaming the manager, or blaming the industry), and not collapsing "what return can this arena structurally sustain" and "is this manager's accountable output what it should be" into one undifferentiated diagnosis. Promotion would require, at minimum: this tension card operator-reviewed; the deciding conditions sharpened into a usable test; and a contradiction-preservation eval in which an internal managerial-output reading and an industry-structure reading of the same operator scenario visibly diverge. No canon candidate exists or is created here, and drafting this card is not itself canon.
