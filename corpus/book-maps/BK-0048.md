## Central thesis

The dominant business-success literature is contaminated by a small set of systematic biases, and the most load-bearing — the halo effect — is the tendency for general impressions of a company's performance to determine perceptions of its specific traits: when a company is doing well its culture is described as strong and its leaders as decisive; when it falters, the same traits get re-coded as rigid and reckless. The corollary biases compound — correlation read as causation, single-cause explanations, winners as templates, methodologies that look rigorous but over-collect from halo-tinted sources. The contribution is not to dismiss the search for what makes companies succeed but to argue that the dominant case-study methodology cannot produce what it claims to produce. (Locators: Preface; Chapter 4; Chapter 9.)

## Argument structure

- **The halo effect is the master bias that contaminates business-success research.** General performance impressions drive perceptions of specific traits, then the trait-level data is used post-hoc to explain the performance. (Chapter 4.)
- **The same case studies produce opposite trait inventories under different performance conditions.** The Cisco and ABB chapters demonstrate how identical-looking traits get re-described across the rise-and-fall cycle. (Chapters 2–3.)
- **Famous best-practice books are constructed from already-halo-tinted source material.** Surveys, journalism, and interviews are selected for and re-coded against the company's known performance trajectory. (Chapters 5–6.)
- **The corollary delusions compound the halo effect.** Correlation read as causation, single-cause explanations, survivor-bias from winning-dots connection, methodologies that are rigorous-looking but biased-rigorous, absolute-performance metrics that ignore competitive context. (Chapter 7.)
- **Case-study narrative and scientific claim require different epistemic postures.** Conflating them produces the methodological problem the book diagnoses. (Chapter 8.)
- **The cure is not better case-study technique but a different posture.** Probabilistic, comparative, context-aware, and explicit about what cannot be inferred from retrospective successful-company data. (Chapters 9–10.) *(Map inference about the book's prescriptive stance.)*
- **The book does not argue that strategy or execution is unknowable** — only that the dominant literature overstates what its methodology can demonstrate. *(Map inference about the book's framing of its own scope.)*

## Misuse risks

- **Halo-effect-as-vague-label.** Used to dismiss any retrospective analysis regardless of whether the halo's structural preconditions (performance signal driving trait perception) actually apply to the case.
- **Reading the book as case-study nihilism.** The argument is that the dominant methodology overstates what it can demonstrate, not that case studies are useless. Importing the critique as a blanket refusal to read business case histories inverts the argument.
- **Treating Rosenzweig as exonerating the books he critiques.** The critique is methodological; it does not argue that *In Search of Excellence*, *Built to Last*, or *Good to Great* have no value, only that their inferential claims exceed their evidence.
- **Cargo-culting a look-at-failures-too prescription without engaging the deeper issue.** Adding a failure sample to a study does not by itself resolve the halo effect if the trait-coding is still post-hoc.
- **Treating the delusions inventory as a checklist.** The list functions as a coherent epistemic posture; running through the items as a checkbox exercise misses the underlying claim about retrospective inference.
- **Importing the critique to domains where the halo structurally does not apply.** Physics, mathematics, and other fields with non-narrative success criteria are not subject to the halo dynamics in the same way; applying the critique by analogy without testing fit produces misdiagnosis.
- **Using the halo-effect label rhetorically to dismiss inconvenient evidence.** Calling any evidence halo-tinted without a specific structural argument is the misuse mirror of the bias the book diagnoses.
