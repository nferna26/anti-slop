## Central thesis

Competitive strategy is the search for a favorable competitive position in an industry, and both the attractiveness of an industry and a firm's position within it are governed by structure. The book offers analytical techniques: five competitive forces — threat of entry, rivalry among existing competitors, pressure from substitutes, buyer bargaining power, and supplier bargaining power — that together determine an industry's profit potential; three generic strategies — overall cost leadership, differentiation, and focus — by which a firm positions itself against those forces; and tools for competitor analysis, market signals, and competitive moves. Later parts specialize the techniques to generic industry environments and to specific strategic decisions. (Locators: Chapter 1; Chapter 2.)

## Argument structure

- **Industry structure governs profitability.** Five competitive forces collectively determine the profit potential of an industry and the strength of each force shapes strategy. (Chapter 1.)
- **Three generic strategies position a firm against the forces.** Overall cost leadership, differentiation, and focus are the coherent strategic postures; failing to commit to one is the named failure mode. (Chapter 2.)
- **Competitors can be analyzed systematically.** Future goals, assumptions, current strategy, and capabilities combine into a competitor response profile. (Chapter 3.)
- **Markets carry signals and moves are deliberate interactions.** Market signals are read, and competitive moves — including commitment — are made as strategic interactions rather than isolated acts. (Chapter 4; Chapter 5.)
- **Structure operates within an industry, not only across industries.** Strategic groups and mobility barriers explain why firms inside one industry earn different returns. (Chapter 7.)
- **The techniques specialize to environments and to decisions.** Part II maps generic industry environments and Part III maps strategic decisions such as vertical integration, capacity expansion, and entry. (Chapter 9; Chapter 14; Chapter 16.)
- **Structural analysis precedes strategy choice.** Map inference, not asserted in any single chapter: the book treats a diagnosed industry structure as prior to position selection, so strategy is chosen against an analysis rather than asserted.

## Misuse risks

- **Five forces as a fill-in template.** The frame is completed as five boxes rather than used as analysis; force strength is asserted without evidence.
- **Generic strategies as a menu.** "Pick cost leadership or differentiation" is treated as a one-off selection rather than a sustained discipline of commitment.
- **"Stuck in the middle" as a slur.** The named failure mode degrades into a label thrown at any firm pursuing more than one thing.
- **The 1980 framing applied without adaptation.** The book's industrial-economics setting is transferred to platform, network, and fast-cycle software markets without flagging where the structural assumptions weaken.
- **Structural determinism.** Industry structure is treated as fixed, ignoring that firms also reshape structure and that BK-0023 itself devotes a chapter to industry evolution.
- **The frame cited without locator.** The five forces are invoked as a bare phrase attributed to Porter without chapter support — the source-lineage failure mode.
